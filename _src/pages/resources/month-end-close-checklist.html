Month-end close checklist

The steps a small business on QuickBooks Online should finish before calling a month closed. Use it as it is, or as a starting point for your own.

Work through the sections in order: cash first, because most other accounts depend on it, and the final review last. Skip what doesn't apply to your business.

1. Cash and bank

  • Reconcile every bank account to its statement.The reconciled balance in QuickBooks should equal the statement's ending balance, with a list of items still uncleared.
  • Reconcile every credit card account to its statement.Include cards held by employees if their charges are recorded in the company's books.
  • Investigate old uncleared items.Checks and deposits uncleared for more than 60 days usually mean a duplicate, a void or a missing entry.
  • Record bank fees and interest.Take them from the statement, not from memory, so the reconciliation closes to the cent.
  • Clear payments waiting to be deposited.Payments to deposit (Undeposited Funds) should hold only receipts that are genuinely in transit at month end.

2. Receivables and revenue

  • Invoice everything delivered in the month.Work completed or goods shipped before month end belong in this month's revenue.
  • Apply unapplied payments and credits.Customer payments sitting unapplied make both the aging and the customer balances wrong.
  • Review the A/R aging.Agree its total to Accounts Receivable on the balance sheet, follow up on old items and decide whether any need a bad-debt allowance or write-off.
  • Record deferred revenue.Amounts billed or collected in advance for work not yet done belong in a liability, not revenue.

3. Payables and expenses

  • Enter every bill dated in the month.Including bills that arrive in the first days of next month for goods or services received this month.
  • Review the A/P aging.Agree its total to Accounts Payable on the balance sheet, and look for duplicate bills and old open items.
  • Accrue expenses incurred but not yet billed.Typical examples: utilities, professional fees, commissions and wages earned but not yet paid.
  • Amortize prepaid expenses.Insurance, software subscriptions and rent paid in advance should be expensed over the months they cover.

4. Payroll

  • Agree payroll expense to the payroll provider's reports.Gross wages, employer taxes and benefits for the month.
  • Reconcile payroll liability accounts.Each balance should equal what is owed and not yet paid. A balance that keeps growing usually means payments are booked to the wrong account.

5. Fixed assets and loans

  • Add new asset purchases to the fixed-asset register.Capitalize purchases above your policy threshold rather than expensing them.
  • Record depreciation and amortization.The month's charge should agree to the register, for every asset in service, including ones added this month.
  • Record disposals.Remove the cost and accumulated depreciation of anything sold or scrapped, and record any gain or loss.
  • Split loan payments into principal and interest.Agree each loan balance to the lender's statement, and accrue interest owed but not yet paid.

6. Inventory and sales tax

  • Agree inventory to a count or valuation.Record the adjustment to cost of goods sold, with the count sheet as support.
  • Agree sales tax payable to the returns.The liability should equal what is due for the periods not yet paid.

7. Review and close

  • Tie the trial balance to the general ledger.Same dates, same accounting basis, run at the same time. See trial balance vs general ledger.
  • Clear holding accounts to zero.Uncategorized income and expense, suspense and "Ask My Accountant" accounts should be empty at month end.
  • Give every balance-sheet account a support schedule.A reconciliation, an aging, a register or a statement that agrees to the balance.
  • Review the month's journal entries.Manual entries especially: each should have an explanation, support and an approver.
  • Keep support for material transactions.Invoices, contracts and statements for anything above your materiality threshold, linked to the transaction.
  • Compare the profit and loss with last month and with budget.Explain any large movement before someone else asks about it.
  • Record who reviewed the close, and when.A short sign-off with the open items is enough.
  • Set the closing date in QuickBooks Online.Use a closing date password so later changes to the closed month need a deliberate step.
  • Save the month's reports.At least the balance sheet, profit and loss, trial balance and the reconciliations, as they stood at close.
