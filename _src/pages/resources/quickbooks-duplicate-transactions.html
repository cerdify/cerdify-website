How to find and fix duplicate transactions in QuickBooks Online

A duplicate bill or expense overstates costs and payables, and it often hides until the vendor statement or the bank reconciliation doesn't agree. Here is where duplicates come from and how to clear them.

Where duplicates come from

  • The bank feed adds what a bill already recorded. A bill is entered, then its payment comes through the bank feed and is added as a new expense instead of being matched to the open bill. The cost is now recorded twice, and the bill still shows as open.
  • The same bill entered twice. Usually by two people, or once from email and once from the paper copy.
  • Two sources for one transaction. A connected app or an import creates the transaction, and someone also enters it by hand.
  • Recurring transactions. A recurring template keeps creating a bill or expense after the vendor's real bills have started being entered.
  • Receipts uploaded twice. The same receipt is captured from a phone and from email, and both are added.

How to find them

  1. Turn on the duplicate warnings. In Account and settings, under Advanced, QuickBooks Online can warn when a bill number or check number has already been used. It doesn't catch everything, but it stops the easiest ones.
  2. Run Transaction List by Vendor for the period, and sort by amount within each vendor. Two entries with the same amount close together in date are the first place to look.
  3. Run Unpaid Bills. A bill that stays open after the vendor says it was paid often has a duplicate expense recorded against the bank.
  4. Compare with the vendor's statement. The vendor's own list of invoices and payments is the quickest way to tell a real second bill from a duplicate.
  5. Check the bank reconciliation. An expense that never clears the bank, or a payment that appears twice, points to the same problem.

Same amount and same vendor is a reason to look, not proof. Monthly rent, subscriptions and split payments look alike on purpose. Open the documents before you change anything.

An example

A fictional company enters a $1,185.40 bill from its cleaning contractor on 3 September and pays the contractor from the bank on 10 September. When the bank feed downloads the $1,185.40 payment, it is added as a new expense to Cleaning Expense instead of being matched to the open bill. September cleaning costs are now $2,370.80, and the bill still shows as unpaid.

The fix: undo the added bank transaction, then match it to the open bill, which records the bill payment. Cleaning Expense goes back to $1,185.40, the bill shows as paid, and the bank reconciliation agrees.

How to fix each kind

  • A bank transaction added instead of matched: in Banking, find it in the categorized or reviewed list, undo it, and match it to the open bill or the transaction already recorded.
  • A bill entered twice and not paid: delete or void the duplicate. Voiding keeps a zero-amount record, which some reviewers prefer because it shows what happened.
  • A bill entered twice and paid twice: the vendor now owes you a refund or a credit. Record the vendor credit or the refund, rather than deleting a payment that cleared the bank.
  • A recurring template still running: delete or pause the template as well as the duplicates it created.

If the month is already closed

If the duplicate is in a month you have closed, especially one already reported to a lender or used for a tax filing, changing the closed month changes numbers other people have seen. Many businesses instead record a correcting entry in the current month and note why. Decide with your accountant, and use the QuickBooks closing date so a closed month can't be changed by accident.
