{{crumbs}} How it works

A month-end close review in five stages.

Every Cerdify project is one company and one period. It moves through the same five stages each month, so you always know what has been checked, what is still open and who decided what.

The five stages

Stage 1

Prepare

Connect QuickBooks Online, follow the guided QuickBooks Online export, or upload a trial balance and general ledger. Cerdify checks the files before the project is created.

  • The trial balance and general ledger must be for the same company, period and accounting basis.
  • Cerdify reconciles the trial balance to the ledger, account by account, and shows any difference.
  • Files that can't be read, or that don't agree, are explained rather than silently accepted.
Stage 2

Diagnose

Accounting diagnostics look for data-quality and reconciliation problems in the ledger, and explain in plain language why each one matters.

  • Each result says what was compared, what was found and what would resolve it.
  • Missing information stays visible as missing. It is never treated as zero.
Stage 3

Support

Set a materiality threshold and Cerdify builds a versioned list of the transactions that need evidence. Match invoices, statements and agreements to them, and record what the reviewer concluded.

  • For each transaction, four questions stay separate: is a document linked, is it the right kind of support, how much of the amount does it cover, and what did the reviewer decide.
  • When the threshold or the ledger changes, a new version of the list is made and the earlier one is kept.
Stage 4

Close

Period-close checks compare the ledger with the records behind it and show what is supported, what is missing and what is unclear. Where the evidence supports a correction, Cerdify proposes the journal entry.

  • Checks cover areas such as depreciation and fixed-asset additions and disposals, accruals, unrecorded bills at period end, prepaid expenses, loan interest, payroll tax liabilities, bank and card balances, and entries that look duplicated.
  • A reviewer approves or rejects each proposed entry. Approved entries post to QuickBooks Online, and Cerdify reads each one back to confirm it matches what was approved.
  • Nothing is posted without a person's approval.
Stage 5

Report

Export Excel and PDF reports that show the work performed, the open items and the limits of the review, ready to share with a lender, an outside accountant or your own team.

  • Reports say what was not reviewed as clearly as what was.
  • Review decisions keep who made them and when.

What a finding looks like

A finding names the problem, the amount, the evidence it rests on and what happens next. This one comes from a fictional company's books.

The fixed-asset register shows a delivery truck placed in service on 1 August, with a monthly depreciation charge of $224.92. The August ledger has no depreciation entry for it. Cerdify flags it as a probable exception, links the register and the purchase invoice, and proposes the entry for a reviewer to approve.

What the review does not do

Cerdify organizes a review and keeps its reasoning visible. It does not perform an audit, review, compilation or assurance engagement, and it does not certify your books. A result marked Supported covers only the comparison it describes. Final judgments stay with the people responsible for the books.
